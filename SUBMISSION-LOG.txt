FINDINGS

Email is strictly better than the in-product tool on every axis that was
actually tested.

It retains its own sent copy, so no discipline is required and the capture
cannot be forgotten -- which is precisely how submission 2 was lost. It
acknowledges receipt, so delivery is confirmed rather than assumed. It has no
practical length limit, so the 14,000 character report went whole. And it can
carry a link to this repository for anything longer still.

The in-product tool provided no acknowledgement for either submission made
through it. Whether those two arrived, and in what state, remains unknown and
now probably unknowable.

That asymmetry is worth stating precisely, because it was measured rather
than assumed: four of four email submissions confirmed, zero of three
in-product submissions acknowledged.

On the length limit: a 3,249 character report survived composition and
copying intact through the in-product tool, and a 6,600 character report was
reported as too large for it. The limit sits above 3,249 and at or below
6,600. That bound exists only because one submission was captured, and it is
the only quantitative fact anyone has about this channel.


PROCEDURE

Use email for anything substantial. It captures, confirms, and does not
truncate.

If the in-product tool is used, copy the text FIRST, then submit. The capture
is the fragile step and the irreversible one comes second, which is the wrong
order and the reason submission 2 has no record.

For anything past a few thousand characters, link rather than paste:
https://github.com/stuartsmith78/feedback-reports

WHAT SUBMISSION 9 MEASURES, AND WHAT IT DOES NOT

This is the first entry in this log where a COMPLETE ROUND TRIP was actually
measured rather than assumed. The user sent the text and echoed it back; it
was diffed against the source and matched exactly.

That matters because this repo's founding premise is that the channel retains
no copy of what arrived, that truncation is believed to occur, and that past
truncation is therefore unverifiable and should be assumed. Submission 9 puts
a VERIFIED FLOOR under the channel's capacity at 1,981 characters.

It does not establish a ceiling. One measurement below a limit says nothing
about where that limit sits, and the 1,200-1,500 character target for
abstracts was never a claim about the channel -- it is a claim about what a
reader will actually read. Both remain correct. The assume-truncation posture

because it protects against a reader stopping early as much as a channel
cutting off.
