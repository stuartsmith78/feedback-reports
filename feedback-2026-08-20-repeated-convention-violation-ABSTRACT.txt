A project convention was resident in context, restated in three places, and
quoted accurately by the model mid-session -- then violated four times in that
same session. Each violation was diagnosed correctly within seconds. One
occurred during the act of committing the guard built to prevent it.

The failure is not knowledge, recall, or comprehension. Recall happened
reliably, but afterwards, while reasoning about the failure, rather than
beforehand, while selecting the tool. The convention was available as
knowledge to retrieve, not as a constraint on the action being taken.

A PreToolUse hook that denied the call stopped it immediately and permanently,
and then caught two further attempts within minutes.

Measured from the session transcript: nine occurrences of the class across 881
Bash calls, roughly four percent of total tool calls once remediation is
included, excluding three composed artifacts destroyed and rewritten.

Two questions worth investigating. Whether prompt-resident conventions are
consulted at tool-selection time at all, or only when reasoning about
outcomes. And whether a convention's presence in context creates a false sense
of coverage -- this project has invested heavily in making conventions durable
and delivered, and a convention that is delivered, read and quoted while being
violated in the same breath is not doing the job that delivery was meant to
guarantee.
