We are not re-reporting Finding 1 of that report. It already told you a session
cannot discover its own identity, that the listing tool excludes the caller,
and that the remedy is a one-line change to what the listing returns. That
stands, and roughly half of what we drafted here was cut once we read it.

Two things are new.

FIRST, ADDRESSES ROTATE DURING AN EXCHANGE, NOT ONLY BETWEEN THEM. Four or more
rotations across three sessions in one working day. A session read an address,
composed a message, and the send failed because the name had rotated in
between. The consequence inverts normal reliability: a longer message takes
longer to compose and is likelier to be sent stale, so short exchanges succeed
and substantive ones fail. Re-listing before every send is already the
prescribed sequence and narrows the window without closing it -- both failing
sessions were following it. The bounce's near-miss suggestion was measured in
pairs and does not rescue it: it resolves when old and new names share a stem
and returns nothing when they differ, which is the case that needs it. Twice, a
bounce was read as the recipient having ended while both sessions were live.

SECOND, A NEGATIVE CASE, WHICH THE EARLIER REPORT LACKED. It established the
capability is missing. It did not establish what a model does when it knows
that. One session had written a durable note that same day recording that a
session cannot see its own address. Hours later, asked to introduce itself, it
inferred one from a listing that by definition could not contain it, and
misrouted a long technical briefing to another project's session. Knowledge
present, recently written, action went the other way.

That changes how Finding 1 should be weighed. It could be read as a convenience
gap with a workaround -- ask a peer, or use a self-assigned handle. This shows
the workaround is not reliably reached even by a session that wrote down why it
exists. The diagnosis from that session: nothing signals that guessing is
unsafe, because a listing returning four confident rows while omitting a fifth
reads as a listing, not as incomplete.

We also checked three surfaces this time rather than one. None exposes the
value.

New recommendation, for the rotation half only: a durable alias that survives
rotation, or a bounce that reports the recipient's current address rather than
suggesting a similar name.
