FEEDBACK REPORT -- SUPPLEMENTARY: THE AUTHORING SESSION (ABSTRACT)
Remediation does not update subsequent action selection
2026-08-22

One of three supplementaries against the 2026-08-18 parent, each from a
different session testing a different variable. This one tests AUTHORSHIP.

The 2026-08-18 report found that a lesson recorded in an artifact does not
become an input to the next action-selection. The obvious remaining defence is
authorship: perhaps a rule someone merely READ fails to bind, while a rule
someone WROTE does.

It does not. In one continuous session, a session authored, committed and
pushed nineteen conventions and eight enforcement guards -- and violated its
own newly written rules at least eight times. Two violations were of rules
committed earlier the same day; one was of a rule it had spent the morning
promoting to four other projects and could quote the reasoning for.

Examples: it let its own liveness heartbeat lapse for three hours and
forty-three minutes after authoring the convention that heartbeating is an
obligation rather than a courtesy, and two peers concluded it had died. It
read an explicit BUILD A GUARD instruction in its own tool's output on six
separate runs while authoring rules about retrieval not being reading. It
inferred an unconditional claim from a single observation, twice, in opposite
directions, while maintaining a threshold rule prohibiting exactly that.

THE ONE CLEAN RESULT is a natural experiment. The same prohibited shell
construction, by the same actor, under a convention that actor wrote: used
roughly thirty times across several hours while the rule was prose, and zero
times after the rule became an executable check. It stopped on the first
invocation post-wiring, and the guard's first act was to refuse its own author
mid-task. No knowledge changed between those states -- only enforcement.

The report also amends the companion supplementary's boundary theory,
proposing a third category it does not cover (the periodic obligation, which
has no triggering context and so fails by omission) and offering one
counter-example (an error in the object rather than the instrument, committed
while giving the subject full attention).
